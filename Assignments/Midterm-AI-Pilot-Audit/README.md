# Midterm-Bio-Inspired-AI-Audit-Pilot

This project analyzed ChatGPT through the lens of neuroscience concepts including memory, attention, reasoning, and world models. The audit compared AI behavior to biological systems discussed throughout the course modules. 

## Objectives

- Evaluate ChatGPT using neuroscience-inspired experiments
- Compare AI memory systems to human memory systems
- Analyze selective attention and interference in AI
- Test reasoning and world-model capabilities
- Connect AI behaviors to neuroscience theories from the course
- Build a Brain vs. AI audit framework for future projects

## Topics Covered

- Working Memory
- Memory Interference
- Context Windows
- Selective Attention
- Inattentional Blindness
- Thousand Brains Theory
- Reasoning and World Models
- Pattern Recognition
- Cause-and-Effect Reasoning
- Bio-Inspired Artificial Intelligence

## Experiments Included

### Experiment 1 — Memory and Interference
Tested whether ChatGPT could remember user-provided information after multiple unrelated questions and interference. The experiment compared AI context retention to hippocampal memory consolidation in humans.

### Experiment 2 — Attention and Hidden Instructions
Tested whether ChatGPT would follow hidden instructions embedded inside unrelated text. The experiment explored selective attention and inattentional blindness concepts from neuroscience.

### Experiment 3 — Reasoning and World Models
Tested whether ChatGPT could reason about real-world physics using concepts such as gravity, inertia, friction, and force. The experiment compared AI reasoning to Thousand Brains Theory and distributed world modeling in the brain.

## Reflection

This midterm project helped me better understand both the strengths and limitations of modern artificial intelligence systems when compared to the human brain. By conducting neuroscience-inspired experiments on ChatGPT, I was able to directly observe how AI systems process memory, attention, and reasoning differently from biological systems.

One of the most important observations from the project was that ChatGPT does not truly store memories in the same way humans do. During the memory interference experiment, the AI successfully recalled information after multiple unrelated questions, but only because the information remained inside the active context window. Unlike the human brain, the AI lacks hippocampal consolidation and does not permanently store experiences outside the session. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

The attention experiment also demonstrated interesting similarities and differences between human and artificial attention systems. The AI ignored hidden instructions and focused primarily on the dominant task, which showed a form of selective attention similar to top-down attention processes discussed in neuroscience. However, unlike humans, the AI does not possess true awareness or adaptive attention systems influenced by meaning or personal relevance.

The reasoning experiment was one of the strongest parts of the project because it demonstrated that the AI could apply concepts such as gravity, inertia, friction, and force to modified real-world scenarios. When gravity was removed from the situation, the AI adjusted its reasoning rather than repeating the same answer. This suggested that the model can simulate cause-and-effect relationships using learned conceptual patterns instead of only memorized responses. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

Another major takeaway from this project was understanding how neuroscience concepts can be applied to evaluate modern AI systems. The experiments connected directly to concepts such as memory consolidation, selective attention, inattentional blindness, and Thousand Brains Theory. This project demonstrated how studying biological intelligence can help explain the capabilities and limitations of artificial intelligence systems.

Overall, this project strengthened my understanding of neuroscience-inspired AI and provided valuable insight into how modern AI systems process information. It also showed that while AI can simulate many brain-like behaviors, it still lacks true biological memory, consciousness, and physical world experience found in human intelligence.
