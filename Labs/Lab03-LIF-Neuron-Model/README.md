# Lab03-Exploring-Neural-Information-Processing

This lab explored biological neuron simulations, artificial neurons, and neural information processing.

## Reflection

This lab helped me better understand the differences between biological neurons and artificial neural networks. During the simulations, I observed how biological neurons generate action potentials based on threshold values and input currents. I learned that weak currents were not enough to produce spikes, while stronger currents generated repeated action potentials. This demonstrated the “all-or-none” principle of biological neurons and showed how neurons communicate using spikes over time.

One of the most interesting parts of the lab was observing the refractory period and membrane potential reset after each spike. This made the biological neuron simulation more realistic and showed how timing plays an important role in neural communication. I also learned how biological neurons process information differently from artificial neurons, which rely on mathematical activation functions rather than spikes.

The lab also helped me compare activation functions such as sigmoid, tanh, and ReLU. I learned how artificial neural networks process continuous values instead of discrete spikes and how feedforward networks calculate outputs using weighted sums and activation functions. This comparison showed how artificial neurons are simplified mathematical models inspired by biological systems.

Overall, this lab strengthened my understanding of neural information processing and demonstrated how neuroscience concepts influence the design of artificial intelligence systems. It also showed the importance of spiking behavior, thresholds, timing, and energy efficiency in future bio-inspired AI models such as spiking neural networks.
