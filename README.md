# Joseph Armijo — ITAI 4374: Neuroscience as a Model for AI

Welcome to my ITAI 4374 portfolio repository. This repository contains assignments, labs, projects, reflections, and neuroscience-inspired AI experiments completed throughout the course.

The purpose of this course was to explore how biological intelligence and neuroscience concepts can inspire modern artificial intelligence systems. Throughout the semester, I analyzed how the brain processes information and compared those processes to machine learning models, neural networks, attention systems, perception pipelines, and reasoning systems used in AI.

---

# About This Course

ITAI 4374 focused on the relationship between neuroscience and artificial intelligence. The course explored how concepts from the human brain influence modern AI architectures and machine learning systems.

Topics included:

- Cortical Column Voting
- Thousand Brains Theory
- Neural Information Processing
- Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) Neurons
- Spiking Neural Networks
- Retina and Visual Cortex Processing
- Attention Mechanisms
- Memory Systems
- Reasoning and World Models
- Bio-Inspired Artificial Intelligence

# What I Learned

This course helped me better understand how biological intelligence influences the development of artificial intelligence systems. Through both theoretical discussions and hands-on experiments, I learned how concepts from neuroscience can inspire neural networks, perception systems, memory architectures, and reasoning models used in modern AI.

One of the most valuable parts of the course was comparing biological systems to artificial systems through experiments and simulations. These activities demonstrated both the strengths and limitations of modern AI models when compared to the flexibility, efficiency, and adaptability of the human brain.

The labs and projects also strengthened my understanding of neural information processing, attention systems, memory models, perception pipelines, and neuroscience-inspired machine learning architectures.

Overall, this course provided a strong foundation in understanding how neuroscience and artificial intelligence intersect and how future AI systems may continue to evolve using principles inspired by the brain.
