# Lab04-Simulating-Spiking-Neurons

This lab explored biological neuron simulations, spike generation, neural encoding, and Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) neuron models using Python and Google Colab.

## Objectives

- Implement a Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) neuron model
- Observe membrane potential and spike generation
- Explore how thresholds and input currents affect neuron firing
- Analyze firing rates and spike train behavior
- Understand how neurons encode information using spikes
- Compare biological neurons to artificial neural networks

## Technologies Used

- Python
- Google Colab
- NumPy
- Matplotlib

## Key Concepts

- Action Potentials
- Threshold Activation
- Membrane Potential
- Refractory Periods
- Spike Trains
- Neural Encoding
- Bio-Inspired Artificial Intelligence

## Reflection

This lab helped me better understand how biological neurons process and transmit information through electrical activity and spike generation. By implementing the Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) neuron model in Python, I was able to observe how membrane potential changes over time and how neurons generate action potentials once a threshold is reached.

One of the most interesting parts of the lab was experimenting with different neuron parameters such as threshold values, membrane time constants, and input currents. Small parameter changes created significant differences in firing behavior, which demonstrated how sensitive biological neurons are to electrical input and environmental conditions.

I also learned how neurons encode information through spike timing and firing rates. When sinusoidal and noisy input signals were introduced, the neuron responded by adjusting the timing and frequency of spikes. This showed how neurons can transform continuous analog information into discrete spike patterns while still preserving meaningful information about the original signal.

The firing rate experiments and threshold sweeps also demonstrated how neurons make decisions about whether to activate. Lower thresholds and stronger currents produced more frequent spikes, while higher thresholds reduced firing activity. These experiments helped connect mathematical neuron models to real biological neuron behavior found in the nervous system.

This lab also strengthened my understanding of the relationship between neuroscience and artificial intelligence. Although artificial neural networks simplify neuron behavior mathematically, many AI systems are still inspired by biological principles such as activation, thresholds, and information processing. The lab demonstrated how neuroscience concepts can influence the development of future AI systems such as spiking neural networks and energy-efficient neuromorphic computing.

Overall, this lab improved my understanding of neural information processing, spike-based communication, and neuroscience-inspired AI systems. It showed how simplified computational models can still capture important biological behaviors and provide insight into how intelligent systems process information.
