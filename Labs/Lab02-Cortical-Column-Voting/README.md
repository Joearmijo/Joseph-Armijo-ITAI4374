# Lab02-Cortical-Column-Voting-Simulation

This lab explored cortical column voting simulations and distributed representations in neuroscience-inspired artificial intelligence systems.

## Objectives

- Understand cortical column organization in the brain
- Explore distributed processing and consensus mechanisms
- Simulate voting behavior between cortical columns
- Analyze pattern recognition through distributed representation
- Connect neuroscience theories to AI architectures

## Topics Covered

- Cortical Columns
- Distributed Representation
- Consensus Voting
- Pattern Recognition
- Parallel Processing
- Thousand Brains Theory
- Neuroscience-Inspired AI

## Reflection

This lab helped me better understand how neuroscience concepts can inspire artificial intelligence systems. During the cortical column voting simulation, I learned how groups of cortical columns can work together to form a shared understanding of patterns and sensory information. Instead of relying on a single decision-making unit, the model uses distributed processing and voting between multiple columns to improve accuracy and stability.

One of the most interesting parts of the lab was seeing how different cortical columns can recognize partial information and then combine their outputs to form a larger prediction. This demonstrated how the brain processes information in parallel rather than sequentially. It also showed how AI systems can benefit from distributed representations and collaborative processing strategies.

The lab also helped me understand the connection between neuroscience and machine learning. Many modern AI systems, especially neural networks, are inspired by biological processes found in the brain. By studying cortical columns and voting mechanisms, I gained a better understanding of how biological intelligence can influence future AI architectures.

Overall, this assignment strengthened my understanding of bio-inspired AI and showed how neuroscience principles can improve pattern recognition, learning, and decision-making systems in artificial intelligence.
